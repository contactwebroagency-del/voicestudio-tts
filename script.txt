The bot in charge is the chief of staff. Everything routes through it. It knows your calendar, your priorities, and what you have already decided. Keep each bot narrow. One job, one set of tools, one memory. Give a bot 10 jobs and it forgets what it is for. The handoff is the part most people skip. Say your inbox eats two hours a day. That's 10 hours a week. Over 50 working weeks, that's 500 hours a year. Half a thousand hours, spent sorting. Now give the morning to two bots. The first reads your calendar before you wake. It sends one short brief. Today's meetings, who they're with, what to prepare, and the top three things to get done. Every Monday, add a view for the whole week, so the brief shows the week, not just the day. Keep it under one page. A short brief gets read, a long one gets skipped. The second bot, sorts the mail, and drafts replies in your voice. Using the messages you already sent as the example. Feed it 20 messages you sent and liked, and tell it the ones you would never send. That's how it learns your voice. For the first few weeks, it only drafts. That every Friday, compare what it wrote with what you actually sent, and tell it what you changed. Over time, the drafts need fewer edits. That's the moment to let it send the routine ones, and only the routine ones. You open one short list of decisions, not a full inbox to process. And here's where it stops being about work.
